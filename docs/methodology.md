# Methods and evidence

This is a guide to the methods described in the dissertation, not a record of a new analysis. Page references below mean physical PDF pages, counted from the cover as page 1.

## Research design

The report combines a questionnaire survey, field observations using GPS, and GIS analysis of spatial and attribute data. The main methods chapter begins on PDF page 21. The data-integration appendix begins on page 54.

| Stage | Reported approach | Evidence available in this archive |
|---|---|---|
| Questionnaire | Collect views about waste management and GIS | Aggregated tables and charts; questionnaire appendix on pages 57-59 |
| Field survey | Record observations and locations with GPS | Narrative descriptions and mapped outputs |
| Data integration | Combine spatial layers and attributes in GIS | Methods narrative and appendix |
| Analysis | Summarise responses and interpret spatial patterns | Reported tables, maps, and discussion |
| Recommendations | Relate findings to improvement opportunities | Conclusions and recommendations on pages 42-44 |

## Questionnaire summaries

Tables on PDF pages 30-35 describe respondent characteristics and answers. The role table on page 30 reports 19 GIS specialists, 38 environmental consultants, and 13 waste-management professionals, for a total of 70 respondents.

The table on page 31 reports 11 ineffective, 9 neutral, 24 effective, and 26 strongly effective responses. These counts total 70. The percentages in the report sum to 100.0% after rounding.

The questionnaire tables provide aggregate results. Individual responses, recruitment records, collection dates, and analysis files are not included here, so sampling quality and the calculations beyond these published summaries cannot yet be checked.

## GIS workflow

The report describes combining spatial data with attribute information to interpret the distribution of waste and its relationship with local characteristics. ArcGIS 10.3 is identified in the appendix.

A future reproduction needs the original layers, attribute tables, join fields, coordinate reference systems, processing settings, and map configurations. The software name alone is insufficient to reconstruct the workflow. No coordinate system, layer resolution, or processing parameter has been invented to fill those gaps.

## Interpreting the outputs

The mapping discussion presents spatial variation and potential contextual influences. The questionnaire represents respondents' views. Treat these as different forms of evidence: favourable opinions about GIS do not directly demonstrate improved routing, reduced collection costs, or a causal effect on waste generation.

See [limitations](limitations.md) and the [reproduction inventory](reproducibility.md) before extending the study.
